Please read these before relying on a result.

Results can be wrong

Results are generated by AI and fixed rules, and can be wrong. A photo can’t prove a stone is real.

Not a certificate

A photo and home tests can’t certify a stone. For valuable pieces, ask a gem lab.

Listing checks

Listing checks look for known trade names, wording and warning signs. A clean result doesn’t mean the seller is honest or the stone is genuine; a warning doesn’t mean the seller is dishonest.

Home tests

Home tests are optional and done at your own risk. Some can mark or damage a stone; try them on a hidden spot and follow each test’s safety note. Keep acetone away from flames and use it in a ventilated room.

Traditional meanings

Meanings are cultural and spiritual traditions, not scientific or medical claims. Crystals are not a substitute for medical care.

Stone information

Descriptions, history, where stones are found and typical prices come from public references (listed in Settings → Content sources) and can contain mistakes. Map pins mark a region, not a mine. Typical prices are rough ranges, not appraisals.

Safety

Never put any stone in drinking water or use it to make “crystal water” or elixirs. Follow the care warnings for toxic or fragile stones, and keep them away from children and pets. Care information is general guidance; if a stone or its dust is swallowed or causes a reaction, contact a doctor or a poison center.

Not affiliated

The app isn’t affiliated with any seller, marketplace or social platform it mentions, such as TikTok, Etsy, Temu, Shein or Amazon. Their names are used only to describe where stones are bought.
